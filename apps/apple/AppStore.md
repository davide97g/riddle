# The App Store submission

Everything App Store Connect asks for, in the order it asks, with the
answers. One app record holds both platforms: the iPhone/iPad build and the
Mac build share the bundle id `com.davide97g.riddle`, so it is one purchase
(free) on both.

Building and uploading is `apps/apple/release.sh --upload`; the screenshots
are `apps/apple/screenshots.sh`. What the app keeps is
[docs/privacy.md](../../docs/privacy.md); the public policy is
`apps/site/privacy.html`.

horror or medical content; no unrestricted web access; no user-to-user
messaging. The one honest *yes* is that the replies are **generated by an AI
model** and are not moderated by the app; answer the AI / generated-content
questions accordingly and accept the rating the questionnaire produces.

**Encryption.** Answered by `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = NO` in the
Info.plist (HTTPS and one HMAC, both exempt), so no question per upload.

**EU (Digital Services Act).** App Store Connect asks whether you are a
trader. A free app with no monetisation from an individual is normally
*not a trader*; if you declare yourself one, your address and phone are
shown on the EU product page.

## App Privacy

**Data Not Collected.**

The reasoning, for the day someone asks: Apple's "collect" means sent off
the device where the developer or a partner can get at it. The app talks to
exactly one place, the server whose address the user types in, which is
software the user runs on their own machine; nothing reaches the developer.
The developer's demo server is for App Review only (its password is in the
review notes and nowhere public) and holds invented pages. If the demo is
ever offered to the public, this answer changes to: Audio Data, User Content
(photos, other) and Other Data, not linked to identity, not used for
tracking, purpose App Functionality.

The privacy manifest (`Riddle/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`) says the same: no
tracking, no collected types, and two required-reason APIs (UserDefaults
CA92.1, system boot time 35F9.1).

## The version page (iOS 1.0.0, and macOS 1.0.0)

**Promotional text (170)**

> Write a question on your e-ink tablet and pause. Your ink fades, and the diary writes its answer back underneath, in its own hand, one stroke at a time.

**Description**

> Riddle Diary is the companion app for riddle, an open-source diary for your e-ink tablet that writes back.
>
> You write a question on the page by hand. You pause. Your ink fades away, and the diary answers underneath it, in its own handwriting, stroke by stroke, with the tablet's own pen engine. Nothing is installed on the tablet but a tiny helper, and nothing about your notebooks changes.
>
> This app is the other half, on your iPhone, iPad and Mac:
>
> • The whole diary as a timeline: what you wrote, as you wrote it, and every answer.
> • Speak to it: hold the microphone and ask out loud. What you say becomes part of the question.
> • Type a note, and send it to be answered on the page.
> • Live: see the tablet's page on your phone, as it is right now.
> • Put a PDF, an ebook or a photo on the tablet straight from your phone.
> • Start and stop the diary, and wipe a page, from wherever you are.
>
> YOU NEED YOUR OWN SERVER
> riddle runs on a small computer of yours at home (a mini PC, a Raspberry Pi, a NAS or a Mac) that talks to the tablet over your own network. The app connects to that server and nothing else: there is no account, no subscription and no server of ours. Setting it up takes one command and a few questions; the guide is at get-riddle.davideghiotto.it/setup.
>
> Works with the reMarkable 2. The reMarkable 1 is experimental; the Paper Pro is not supported.
>
> The replies are written by an AI model that your server calls with your own API key (OpenAI by default, or any compatible provider). Your server sends it a picture of what you wrote and what was said near the microphone, to write the answer. Everything else stays on your server.
>
> riddle is free and open source (MIT). Not affiliated with reMarkable AS; reMarkable is a trademark of reMarkable AS.
